01 — THE TRANSMISSION

“If you're hearing this… don't come looking for us.”

Then the signal died.

During The Blackout, Port Harcourt went dark in a single second. By morning, the people were gone — from Rumuola to Borokiri, from Diobu to Eleme. Cars stood with their doors open. Pots were still on the fire. Phones lay in the streets, still ringing.

You enter the city searching for the missing. Their journals, voice notes and photographs point to the same thing: a group of investigators had been mapping strange structures beneath the city — structures older than the city itself.

The Blackout was not an accident. Someone intentionally opened something that should have remained buried.

07 — THE FIVE-CHAPTER RELEASE

One survivor. Five chapters.

Single-player campaign

Investigate Rumuola, survive Slaughter Market, cross the Flooded Mile, escape Diobu and face the source beneath Rumuokoro.

Play in your browser

Use a desktop or laptop with a keyboard and mouse. Headphones are recommended. The game contains flashing lights, sudden sounds and horror combat.

Controls

WASD moves, Shift sprints and E interacts. Later chapters introduce combat controls, shown when you acquire each weapon. In chapter five, G draws or holsters, X reloads, H heals and Q dashes.

Future chapters

This release tells a complete five-chapter story. Future expansion will depend on player feedback. Multiplayer is not included.

08 — BENEATH THE CROSSROADS

Follow the signal to its source.

The recordings connect the missing people, the silent houses and the attacked convoy. The final chapter brings the investigation beneath Rumuokoro. What is waiting there has learned how to call you closer.

09 — ORIGINAL SCORE

Silence is the warning.

An original soundtrack built on the feeling of 1990s–2000s Nigerian horror: low distorted drums, old-style percussion patterns, haunted vocal textures, radio distortion and dark synths. No samples. No borrowed audio.

The music goes almost completely silent right before major encounters. When it stops — run.
